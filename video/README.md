# Chapter intros: 2–3 minute riso explainers

One short film per chapter, 2–3 minutes long, narrated by a MiniMax HD voice (English_magnetic_voiced_man, via Atlas Cloud; the author-voice clone remains available with --engine clone) and animated as a risograph print with [riso-motion](https://github.com/schlunsen/riso-motion). riso-motion is built on John Heibel's ClaudeAnimationBase (MIT). Each film tells the chapter's best story, gives the listener three takeaways and hands off to the next chapter.

**Done:** all 21 films (chapters 1–19, appendices A and B), live at https://theagenticcrew.com/watch. YouTube upload copy: `youtube.md` (channel, Ch 1–2) and `youtube-videos.md` (Ch 3–B, generated by `tools/youtube_md.py`).

**Tone:** explain the chapter's ideas plainly and honestly. No first-person anecdotes: the narrator from Chapter 2 on is an AI voice, so "I did this" stories would put the author's experiences in someone else's mouth.

## Pipeline

```
narration/<ch>.json ──voice.py──▶ build/<ch>/beats/*.wav, voice.wav, timing.json ──┐
                                   riso/src/scenes/<ch>.timing.js  ◀──────────────┘
storyboards/<ch>.md ──(by hand/agent)──▶ riso/src/scenes/<ch>.js + riso/<ch>.html
music bed (assets/bgm-hazelwood.mp3) ──mix.sh──▶ build/<ch>/mix.m4a
riso/render.mjs --page=<ch>.html --frames → --encode --audio=mix.m4a ──▶ build/<ch>/<ch>-<slug>.mp4
```

1. **Script** (`narration/<ch>.json`) has 7–9 beats, about 380–450 words, which comes to 2:20–2:50. It is written in the first person and stays close to the chapter's own lines.
2. **Voice** (`tools/voice.py <ch>`) uses MiniMax Speech 2.6 HD (`English_magnetic_voiced_man`) on Atlas Cloud by default. `--engine clone` switches to a local Qwen3-TTS clone of `assets/voice-ref.wav`. Each take is transcribed by Whisper and re-generated if it drifts from the script. The script writes word-level timings. Re-take a single beat with `--redo <beat>`.
3. **Storyboard** (`storyboards/<ch>.md`) gives one shot per beat. Each shot has an event keyed to a spoken word, a transition at every seam, and the helm motif.
4. **Scene** (`riso/src/scenes/<ch>.js`) calls `wt(beat, word)` for every cue, so re-voicing a beat moves the animation with it. Shared pieces live in `common.js`: the ident, the end card, the helm, cards, stamps and seams. The Skipper lives in `src/skipper.js`, with named poses and `skipAct()`; his model sheet is `cast.html` (`--loop=cast`).
5. **Check** with contact sheets (`node render.mjs --page=<ch>.html --sheet=…`), then render. On an M3 Pro a full render takes about 3–4 minutes with 4 workers.
6. **Mix** (`tools/mix.sh <ch> <bed> [reference.wav]`) masters the voice to -16 LUFS, with a -1.5 dBFS ceiling. With a reference, `tools/voicematch.py` solves the EQ and gain on the finished signal until bass, mids and treble match that recording; Chapter 1 uses `assets/voice-ref.wav`. `tools/tonal.py` prints the band-by-band comparison. The bed sits at -30 LUFS and ducks under the voice by sidechain compression, with fades in and out.

Needs: Node, Chrome, ffmpeg, and a Python 3.12 env with `mlx-audio mlx-whisper soundfile numpy`. Put `ATLASCLOUD_API_KEY` in your environment for music. The key is never written to the repo.

## Series look

- **Inks:** Federal Blue + Orange + Yellow, which prints the cover's navy and gold. Gold comes from overprinting orange on yellow, green from federal on yellow, and brown from federal on orange.
- **Cast:** Clawd is the agent. The Skipper is the engineer: a bearded captain in a navy peacoat with brass buttons and a white cap.
- **Motif:** the ship's wheel from the cover. It assembles in every ident, and every film ends on a "NEXT" card with the helm turning.
- **Type** appears only in the ident, the end card, and one number per film when the number *is* the point ("100", "500,000").
- **Music:** `assets/bgm-hazelwood.mp3`, mixed low. Two MiniMax Music takes came back with vocals despite the instrumental flag, so run any new bed through a Whisper check before using it.

## Publishing

`python3 video/tools/publish.py [chXX …]` web-encodes each finished master to `website/public/films/` (1080p, CRF 29, tune grain), writes a poster and captions (WebVTT from the word timings), and updates `website/src/data/films.json`. The `/watch` pages, the per-film pages and the homepage chapter links read that file. Then run `just deploy-fast`.

## Making more films with agents

`AGENT_BRIEF.md` is the brief the film agents followed. Lessons from making 19 films in parallel on one laptop:
- **Run at most 2–3 agents at once.** Each render drives headless Chrome, and six at once pushed the load past 100 and made agents stall.
- **Only encode after the render has finished,** when the frame count equals round(duration × 24). Encode to a temp file and move it into place. One film was encoded mid-render, and another was corrupted by two encodes writing the same file.
- **Long titles overflow `ident()` and `endCard()`.** The chapter files draw their own two-line title cards. A future fix is to make the shared helpers wrap.

## Where they live on the site

- **Homepage "The voyage":** add a small ▶ on each chapter row in `Voyage.astro` that opens the intro in a lightbox. The data goes in an optional `intro` field in `website/src/data/chapters.ts`.
- **A `/intros` page:** all films in a riso-styled grid, plus the transcript, which is the narration JSON rendered as text for accessibility and SEO.
- **Captions:** `timing.json` already has word times, so a WebVTT file per film is a small script away.
- **Hosting:** the MP4s are about 20–40 MB each. Keep them out of git. The production host or a CDN can serve them, or use YouTube/Vimeo with the site embedding them.
- **Formats:** 16:9 for the site and YouTube. A 9:16 cut of the hook beat alone (about 30 s) would work for social; the scene would need a portrait layout pass.

## Effort per chapter (after the pilot)

A script is about 20 min to write and review, and the voice about 5 min of machine time. The storyboard and scene take 1–2 h with an agent, including the contact-sheet review loop. Render and mix take about 5 min, and music costs $0.15. The bulk of the work is the scene code, and `common.js` grows with every chapter.
